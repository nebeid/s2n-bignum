Two diagnostic variants used to separate the two causes of the cascade's loss
(r8g, Neoverse-V2 2.7925 GHz, 40 reps, ns/call, min):

`cascnk` = the naive cascade (`casc`, seven `ldp` round-key reloads per
one-block section) with those 56 key loads MECHANICALLY DELETED.  Functionally
wrong on purpose (SELFCHECK fails at every nblk, as expected) and run under
ALLOW_MISMATCH=1: it is a timing-only lower bound on "cascade with free key
loads".  It lands within 0.6% of the properly key-hoisted `casck`, which is why
`casck` is used as the primary cascade everywhere in the report.

`casc3` = the naive cascade with adjacent sections paired so the second of each
pair folds both blocks' products with three `eor3` instead of two sets of three
`eor` (12 slots saved at nblk=8).  Worth 1-3%: 35.360 vs 36.234 ns at nblk=8.
Not carried forward: it does not change the conclusion and complicates the
entry stubs (odd entry points must zero the unrun first-of-pair products).

`casc2` = width-2 cascade with hoisted keys (gen_casc2.py), .text 7608 B:
 nblk=1..8 ns on r8g: 12.594 13.296 14.610 17.454 20.900 23.627 26.966 29.767
It is within 1.5% of the uniform-generator `cw2` (which rotates keys), so the
width series measured with gen_cascW.py is not distorted by its key handling.
